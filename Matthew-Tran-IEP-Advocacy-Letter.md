## 2. Why: The Assessment Data Shows the Current Group Model Is Not Reaching Matthew

The District's own **Multidisciplinary Psycho-Educational Assessment Report (12/9/2025)** and the **IEP present levels** document a child who *can* learn but is **not accessing the current group-based instruction** — and who learns markedly better one-on-one and through visual/technology supports.

**He learns through individualized, visual, technology-based instruction — not whole-group/verbal instruction:**
- The school psychologist wrote that Matthew **"responded well to visual prompts on the iPad but struggled with verbal responses,"** "engaged best when seated in the computer chair," and that the test results **"do not reflect an accurate representation of Matthew's cognitive abilities"** because of distractibility and difficulty following *verbal* directions.
- **Father's interview answer:** *"He needs one-on-one teaching at school… He learns new things very quickly."*
- The classroom observation (11/4/25) shows the problem plainly: during whole-group/choral math instruction Matthew **kept his head down, sang off-topic, and did not participate in choral responses or maintain visual attention.** But once an aide worked with him **1:1 using modeling, verbal cues, and prompt fading,** he completed the work — and **"when prompts were reduced, he showed the ability to complete the work independently."**

**The conclusion is the District's own:** the report states Matthew **"requires smaller group instruction with more access to adult support."** We are asking the IEP to make that concrete and individualized.

**He is years behind and, in key areas, not making progress under the current model:**
- 5th grade, but academic levels are roughly **1st grade**: Broad Reading **≈1.3 GE**, Broad Math **≈1.1–1.5 GE**, Broad Written Language **≈1.6 GE** (Woodcock-Johnson). Reads ~**40–60 words/minute**.
- **Two of his prior IEP goals were NOT met — both in math** (Addition/Subtraction and Multiplication), per the IEP team's own progress review (12/9/2025 meeting notes).

**His communication and adaptive needs are severe and central:**
- Verbal Comprehension Index **45**, Working Memory **45**, Processing Speed **60** (WISC-V, all Extremely Low).
- Receptive Language Index **45** (TACL-4); Expressive Language Index **47** (TEXL); Pragmatics **0.4 percentile** (CELF-5).
- Adaptive behavior (ABAS-3) General Adaptive Composite **47 (parent) / 66 (teacher)** — Extremely Low; **Communication** and **Functional Academics** at the lowest scaled scores.
- He still needs **1–2 prompts to request wants/needs** and **sometimes uses maladaptive behavior to communicate.**

> **Legal basis:** An IEP must be **"reasonably calculated to enable a child to make progress appropriate in light of the child's circumstances"** (*Endrew F. v. Douglas County*, 2017). When a child does **not make expected progress** on goals, the IEP team **must revise the IEP** (34 C.F.R. § 300.324(b)(1)(ii)(A)). **Specially designed instruction** means adapting the **content, methodology, AND delivery** of instruction to the child's unique needs (34 C.F.R. § 300.39(b)(3)). The data above is exactly the kind of "lack of expected progress" and learning-style mismatch the law requires the team to address.

## 3. Matthew's Strengths — Build Instruction On These

The reports are consistent about what works for Matthew. Individualized instruction should be designed around these strengths:

- **Technology and visual learning:** responds to **iPad and computer-based** prompts; learns by **watching and copying videos/tutorials**; highly motivated by computers, video creation, and "creative alphabet videos."
- **Rote/visual memory:** strength in rote memorization of numbers, letters, sight words (reads ~**350 sight words**), musical lyrics, and schedules; **number sense and math facts** noted as relative strengths.
- **Visual-spatial reasoning** is his highest cognitive area (VSI = 81, Low Average) — far above his verbal scores. **He takes in information visually far better than verbally.**
- Works hard, responds to reinforcement, enjoys music/PE, and is described as a happy, capable child who "is very capable of doing anything he is interested in."

The takeaway: **deliver instruction visually and 1:1, using technology, and his learning accelerates.** This is the single most important theme across every section of the assessment.

## 4. Specific Requests for the IEP Team

We propose the team discuss and document the following (the team makes final decisions together; these are our proposals):

### A. Increase dedicated individualized (1:1) instruction
- Add **specified 1:1 instructional minutes** for **reading, math, and writing** within the school day — not just incidental aide support — since the data shows Matthew progresses with **1:1 modeling/prompting/fading** and stalls in whole-group/choral instruction.
- **Re-examine the two not-met math goals** (addition/subtraction with regrouping; multiplication): change the **method of instruction** (1:1, visual, technology-based, explicit and repeated practice), not just the target date.
- Reduce reliance on whole-group/choral teaching for core academics; document how and when individualized instruction will be delivered.

### B. Assistive Technology / AAC — assess and provide
- Conduct an **Assistive Technology assessment**, including evaluation for an **AAC / iPad-based communication and instruction system.** Given VCI = 45, severe expressive/receptive language, and his strong response to iPad/visual input, AT is a clear, data-supported need.
- Affirmatively address the IEP's **AT "special factor"** consideration (34 C.F.R. § 300.324(a)(2)(v)) and provide the device, software, and training Matthew needs to access instruction and communicate (34 C.F.R. § 300.105).

### C. Strengthen speech-language services
- Increase and/or **individualize** speech-language services (currently 6 × 30 min/month). Given **Receptive 45, Expressive 47, Pragmatics 0.4 %ile**, request a mix of **individual** therapy plus **push-in support for functional communication** across the day, paired with the AAC system above.

### D. Behavior support that protects instructional access
- **Review/update the Functional Behavior Assessment (FBA) and Behavior Intervention Plan.** The assessment documents **elopement (~2×/week)**, **self-injury (hitting his head)** during a worksheet, and **mouthing/shirt-chewing** when stressed — behaviors that directly remove Matthew from instruction and raise safety concerns.
- Consider **more than the current 30 min/month behavior-specialist consult** — e.g., direct behavior support and staff coaching — so individualized instruction can actually be delivered.

### E. Middle-school transition planning
- Matthew is in his final elementary year. Request a **written transition plan** for the move to middle school in fall 2026 (campus visits, staff introductions, visual supports, and an **elopement/safety plan** for a larger campus and new routines). His behavioral rigidity and elopement make a planned transition essential.

### F. Use Matthew's strengths and don't cap him by invalid scores
- Direct that instruction be **strengths-based and technology-mediated** (visual models, video, iPad, his interest in letters/computers).
- Because **multiple test scores were flagged by the assessors as underestimates** due to dysregulation, ask the team to program for Matthew's **demonstrated ability with 1:1 support** (where he completes work independently once prompts fade), not the floor scores.
